Research Profile

Dr. ElSaadani is a Research Assistant Professor in Civil Engineering at the University of Louisiana at Lafayette and a Research Project Manager at the UL Lafayette Watershed Flood Center, part of the Institute for Coastal and Water Research. His work centers on hydrologic modeling, environmental remote sensing, machine learning applications in hydrology, and geospatial statistical modeling of stream networks. He has led multidisciplinary studies that integrate advances in computer science, remote sensing, and large-scale data analytics to improve current practices in hydrologic science.

His recent efforts focus on community resilience through flood modeling, UAV observations, sensor networks, and decision-support tools that help local and state partners quantify flood risk, evaluate evacuation scenarios, and strengthen emergency-response capabilities across south Louisiana.

Research Interests

Hydrologic Prediction, Remote Sensing, and Flood Risk Analysis

Hydrologic and Hydrodynamic Modeling

Distributed rainfall-runoff modeling, channel routing, floodplain hydraulics, design-storm characterization, flood exposure analysis, and structure-damage estimation.

Remote Sensing and Geospatial Statistics

Radar and satellite precipitation products, uncertainty characterization, stream-network geostatistics, environmental remote sensing, and hydroclimatic data assimilation.

Machine Learning and Decision Support

Spatiotemporal machine learning, UAV and sensor observations, cloud-GIS flood monitoring, and decision-support workflows for community-driven flood risk management.

Notable Project Leadership Highlight

Louisiana Watershed Initiative Technical Design and Quality

Dr. ElSaadani served on the LWI Technical Design and Quality (TDQ) Team, supporting technical review, monitoring-network design, and quality assurance for statewide watershed modeling and flood risk analysis.

  • Led LWI monitoring network design and placement efforts in coordination with the USGS.
  • Participated in quality assurance of model-design efforts under LWI Task Order 3 (TO3).
